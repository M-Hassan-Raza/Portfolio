I'm Hassan. I run product and engineering at [Entropy Labs](https://entropylabs.dev), a team of about a dozen building Obelisk, October and software for clients. The title on the door says CIO. Most days that means deciding what we build next, then staying close enough to the code to know whether the plan survives contact with it.

The work I like best sits in the unglamorous middle: the stock count that has to stay right when two cashiers sell the last item at once, the tenant boundary nobody should be able to cross, the agent that needs a hard stop before it spends someone's budget. I've been doing some version of that since university, when client work for retail shops turned into [Polaris](/projects/polaris/) and taught me that the edge cases are the product.

Outside work I send fixes to the open-source tools I use. That's [{{< oss-count >}} merged pull requests](/open-source/) so far, mostly in kitty, calibre, libtorrent, Fallow and pyscn. I also taught university labs at FAST-NUCES for a few years, and I still think the fastest way to understand a system is to break it and then explain why it broke.

For the record, Entropy Labs won Ignite's acceleration round, had financing approved under Accelerate Prosperity, and exhibited at GITEX 2025 and LEAP 2026.
